Starter deck (1 vs 1)

The 9 robots players draft at the start of a game (see 3, keep 1, twice). 11 stat points, no stat above level 3, stock wheels and controller, no module or helpers: everything else comes from upgrade cards. Level 4 in speed, turret or firepower adds overdrive strain, so upgrades are where engineers start to matter. Row against column, 200 fights each.

  • With 3 or 4 robots each one shoots its nearest enemy and switches when another gets clearly closer. Friendly fire is on: stray bullets, mortar splash (the shooter's own too) and the laser hurt teammates. Robots hold fire when a teammate is right in the line of fire, but spread and movement still cause accidents. The laser beam goes through every robot in its path.
  • Luck: every hit has a 10% chance to be a critical (double damage).
  • Strain and engineers: every part puts strain on a robot (weapon 1–3, heavy calibre 1, module 0–2, each helper 1, each stat at level 4+ 1). More strain means a bigger chance of a short malfunction (engine stall or jammed turret) and of a part breaking for the rest of the fight (weapon damaged, engine blown, module fried, drone failure...). Each engineer (0–3 per robot) cuts the malfunction chance by 40%, halves the break chance and shortens malfunctions.
  • Bookie's board: before each fight the bookie simulates 40 fights of what everyone can see (hidden modules are left out when "Hide modules" is ticked) and posts fuzzy bands instead of exact odds: Heavy favourite, Favourite, Even, Underdog, Long shot. Results are cached, so a matchup you have seen before shows up instantly.
  • Drives (open info): Standard Wheels, Tank Tracks (slower, turns fast, half the weight and knockback), Omni-Drive (glides sideways, light), Overboost Thrusters (fast with a dash, wide turns). Processors change how a robot moves: Zigzag (weaves, dodges shots), Kiter (keeps its distance, backs away while firing), Rammer (drives into its target and rams it). Both add strain.
  • New modules: Reflective Plating (half of each laser hit bounces back, slower to catch fire), Grappling Hook (yanks the target closer), Jump Jets (leaps away from danger, untouchable mid-air), Oil Slick (puddles that make robots slide; flames set them alight). Railgun (legendary weapon): an instant slug that pierces every robot and wall in a 450 px line and ignores half the armour.
  • Fight recap: after each fight, the key moments (knockouts, broken parts, malfunctions, big crits, flanks, module moments) and the turning point, the moment the winner took the lead for good.
  • Squadron battle: each side plays a squadron leader card. Lone Wolf = 1 titan (28 points, stats up to 8, bigger), Duo = 2 × 18 points (up to 6), Trio = 3 × 14 (up to 4), Swarm = 5 × 9 (up to 3, small). Budgets were tuned with simulations so the cards are roughly even; the Lone Wolf is the underdog and beats a Trio about 4 times in 10.
  • Every robot spends its points over 7 stats, then picks a gun calibre, a preferred range, and a weapon (14 points, levels 0–4 outside squadron battles).
  • Rapid guns: many small bullets, quick turret. Armour removes a flat amount from every hit, so it shrugs off rapid fire.
  • Heavy guns: big slow shells and a slow turret that needs precise aim. Armour and health add weight, which costs speed.
  • Helpers (up to 3 per robot) are open information: Medic (heals, fragile), Waller (energy walls), Bodyguard (soaks bullets), Spotter (aim + reveals modules), Jammer (slows enemy turrets), Gun Drone (small gun), Bomber (one-shot dive bomb), Miner (mines). 6 or 8 robots means 3 vs 3 or 4 vs 4 in a bigger arena. If the main robot dies, its helpers shut down. "Shoots at" is the secret targeting order.
  • Modules (one per robot) are the surprise cards: Shield, Flipper, Decoy, Mines, Repair Drone, EMP, Self-Destruct, Smoke. Tick "Hide modules" to see them the way an opponent would: just a ? until the module reveals itself.
  • Spinner blade: the whole robot spins and rams; on contact a big hit that throws the enemy back; a quarter of bullets and saw blades glance off. Flamethrower: short cone of fire that sets robots alight; burning ignores armour (5 per second) and spreads to any robot they touch, teammates included.
  • Laser: ignores armour, short range, has to keep its beam on the target. Mortar: lobbed shell with splash, can't fire up close. Saw blades: bounce off the arena edge, energy walls and robots, up to 3 hits per blade, and never hurt their own squad.
