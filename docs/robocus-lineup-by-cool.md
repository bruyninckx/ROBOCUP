# ROBOCUS lineup by coolness, tags, and how many levels (proposal, 14 Nov 2026)

Kev (14 Nov): "we need to re-organize robots based on how cool they look and we need to decide based on game dynamics if we need more levels in the auction or if that is not needed, if we have too many levels we just pick the most fun robots." And: "figure out what tags we can give all these robots, that will also be part of how cool they are."

Picture: `robocus-lineup-by-cool.png` (every robot in its proposed level, with its tags and quirk).

## 1. More levels? No: keep starters + 4 levels, and pick the most fun robots

What the game uses (2.0 tabletop sim, 60 games, 8-9 Nov defaults):

| | |
|---|---|
| rounds per game | about 11-12 |
| starter draft | 4 teams see 2 each, so 8 of the 9 starters show up |
| robots sold per game | level 1: 2.1, level 2: 2.1, level 3: 3.6, level 4: 5.8 (about 14 market robots) |
| when each level comes in | level 1 from round 1, level 2 from round 2, level 3 from round 4, level 4 from round 6 |
| one level | wins about 60% against the level below |
| fame from fights | 69% of all fame |

Why not more levels:
- **The waves already fill the game.** A new level every 2-3 rounds over 12 rounds is a steady climb. A fifth market level would either come in so late it hardly gets played, or squeeze the others together.
- **A wider top gap makes the game worse.** The 9 Nov test with a bigger gap between levels gave more predictable fights and fewer close finishes; with 69% of fame coming from fights, that is where games are decided.
- **Finer levels would not be felt.** Fights are random enough (the bookie's favourite wins about 69%) that a level much smaller than 10 points would be invisible.

How many robots each level needs: about 2-3 per game are sold at levels 1-2, 4 at level 3 and 6 at level 4 (all 8 level 4 robots are dealt each game). So 9 starters, 5 at level 1, 5 at level 2, 6 at level 3 and 8 at level 4 = **33**. We have 44: the 11 least fun sit on the bench. A level can carry 1-2 extra robots for variety between games (the game deals only part of each deck), so a benched favourite can come back without changing the rules.

## 2. Tags

Every robot gets:
- **One weapon family**: Spinner, Saw, Hammer, Flail, Puncher, Piercer, Crusher, Grabber, Flipper, Lifter, Rammer, Arm, Tail, Drill, Magnet, Flame, Shooter, Harpoon, Thrower, Jumper, Dropper, Legs, Carousel.
- **One range**: Melee (touching), Reach (up to about 1.5 m: arms, flails, cables), Ranged (shoots or throws across the arena).
- **Spectacle tags** (what you see happen on screen): **Charges up** (a visible wind-up you can read), **Throws** (things fly: robots, records, junk), **Flies** (leaves the floor), **Grabs** (holds its victim), **Fire**, **Multi-part** (several moving parts), **Arena-shaper** (leaves things in the arena), **Recycles** (picks its ammo back up), **Explodes**, **Traps**, plus **Smart** (reads the enemy and picks its moment; not counted for coolness).
- The counter tags the game already shows stay as they are (strong vs / weak vs).

**Cool score (1-6)** = looks (1-3, from the snapshot gallery: silhouette, size of the weapon, lamps) + spectacle tags (one point each, up to 3).

Levels are filled by cool score only (Kev, 14 Nov: "we will tweak the numbers if necessary"): strength is tuned afterwards, so a robot's level says how cool it is, not how strong it is today. Ties are broken by how fierce and big the weapon looks (Kev: higher levels look fierce).

## 3b. Quirks: one per market robot

1.0 gave every robot card one quirk (Showstopper, Hotshot, Scope, Trick Shot, Grudge, Underrated, Sticky Fire, Treacherous, Fireproof, Bastion, Cunning, Headline Act). The code is still in the 2.0 tabletop, switched off (`quirkOf = () => null`). Bringing them back gives every market robot a personality on top of its weapon, and it is part of what makes it cool. Starters stay without a quirk (as in 1.0). Each quirk is matched to the robot whose character fits:

## 4. What this says

- **Strength is tuned afterwards.** The untweaked ratings (`docs/robocus-untweaked-roundrobin.md`) say how far each robot is from its level; the coolest ones are mostly the furthest (Jukebox, Orbiter, Bricklayer, Scavenger, Slingshot need big boosts at their new levels; Corkscrew and Vertigo are strong for level 1). The honest dials come first (weapon size, motor, armour), a small power grade to finish.
- **Looks:** in the gallery nearly every robot is the same blue box; only the weapon differs. The cheapest cool-up for the whole roster would be its own body shape per robot (round, wedge, long and low, tall, legged), with the team colour kept.
- **These are suggestions to change by hand**: the cool score is a judgement (looks) plus a count (tags), and the quirk pairings are a first match.

The tags and cool scores are not in the game files yet (this is a proposal). Strength numbers: the untweaked round robin, `docs/robocus-untweaked-roundrobin.md`.

## 5. Bookie tags in the new setup (14 Nov, measured)

These are the 2.0 tabletop's bookie tags (`bookieTags` in `prototypes/tabletop-2.html`), re-measured for the 33 robots of the lineup above, untweaked (grade 1, damage x1), with `tools/damage-lab/tag-sim.js` and `tag-report.js` (data: `tag-report-2026-11-14.json`):
- 2,112 duels (every pair 4 times), 360 four-way free-for-alls, 360 2v2s, 950 arena fights (each robot against the same 6 random opponents on the open floor and on each special arena).
- Same rules as the game: **Giant killer** (beats stronger robots at least 1.25x what its strength predicts), **Coin-flip brawler** (50%+ of its fights are blowouts, winner keeps 85%+ health), **Quick finisher** / **Grinder** (fights average 40 s or less / 70 s or more): one of these per robot, in that order. **Hard hitter** (when it loses, the winner loses 6+ points more health than average, 23%). **Brawl master** / **Lost in a melee** (free-for-all wins 10+ points above / below its duel strength). **Lone wolf** / **Team player** (2v2). **Arena** tags (saws, Killbox, Cage, ice: a logit change of 0.45 or more after halving for noise).
- "Stronger" here means a higher untweaked rating, not a higher level: levels are now set by coolness, so the level-based tags (Giant killer, Counter pick, Bad matchup) have to be measured again once the numbers are tuned.
